# U03e — The room card

Asked by the user on 2026-10-01: the room is hard to enjoy with the scroll down to its words and the big buttons at the
end. Judged on the mock `docs/analysis/mocks/room-card.html` (private page
`https://claude.ai/artifact/XdenDW5EwdYyEJxwi8Box5`, version 5), which is this step's look and feel; the game keeps its
own room drawing, top bar and rail (the mock says which parts are stand-ins).
Not here: the game's rules, the street, tower and corridor screens, the buffer screen, the help's words (U07), the
telemetry pane and the scan table's own words (U06).

## Picks (the user's, 2026-10-01)
- **The room is a card.** The front is the picture; a folded corner, or a sideways swipe, turns it to its back: the
  room's words, its relics and every move as rows. No scroll to reach them.
- **Six turns** — flip, door, peel, blinds, static, torn — picked with no order to guess; the two broken ones more often
  as coherence falls.
- **Doorways carry no words.** One into a room already entered spills that room's light and carries its number; one
  into a room not yet entered is a frame full of fog with a slow pulse; the way out keeps its own colour. Smaller posts,
  dimmer light and fog (the last mock round). Relics keep their names.
- **The room's name and first words** show on arrival, then clear.
- **A strip of four keys** in place of the dock: Buffer, Map, Trace, and one that steps back toward the entrance and
  reads Leave in the first room. (Since the fast loop of 2026-10-02, `tasks/ui/U03e-fast-loop.md`: six icon keys —
  MORE, Buffer, Map, Trace, back, forward — and the game's commands on MORE's sheet, not on the back.)

## Plan (amended after the review, 2026-10-01)

### Departures from the mock and the Decisions (Decision 2) — each a pick for the go
1. **The top bar keeps its Buffer count** beside the key's: the bar is the same on every drawn place (U03c's pick) and
   the mock's bar is a stand-in. The relic still flies to the key.
2. **A doorway's number is 12 px in a text ink**, the picture's floor and its inks (`PlanPicture.test.ts:165-169`); the
   mock writes 11 px in the light's colour. Only the light spilling in takes the next room's colour.
3. **A room only seen into** (the plan's `known` sight: a neighbour of a visited room, or any after a scan) has a framed
   doorway with fog, like one never seen: its light and number come only once it is entered. The mock has no such
   state.
4. **At full coherence no turn is broken** (`decay` 0); the mock keeps a small chance. The chance grows evenly with
   `decay` to nine in ten at zero coherence, tuned on the phone.
5. **The room's name leaves the back wall** of the room you stand in, as on the mock (told on arrival and on the back
   of the card); other rooms keep their names on the plan. The other way: it stays written on the wall.
6. **A step that brings a panel** (a scan, the lattice map's refusal) shows the card's back at once, the panel first;
   the mock has no panels.
7. **Decision 1's twin is behind the picture, not under it**: while the front shows, the list is hidden from everyone,
   and the corner — a named button — is the way to it. The four keys stay in reach on both faces.
8. **One button a move**: the way back is the strip's key only and Buffer and Trace are the strip's only; the back of
   the card lists the other moves (the way forward) and the game's other options. The mock lists every way on the back
   as well.
9. **A swipe turns the card only while you stand in the room, or on its back**; over the plan (the Map key pressed) one
   finger pans, as today (`PlanScene.ts:39-41`), and the corner still turns.

### 1. The engine — a room's light on the plan
`PlanRoom` gains `light`, the key of the list a room's lighting came from, on every room, so nothing narrows on its
sight; the look that paints a doorway is the only reader. `Apartment.plan` (`Apartment.ts:154-172`) asks each child
through a new `Location.light()` (a place with no lighting answers the empty key; `Room` its `RoomLook`'s). No state
is added, so the save is untouched.

### 2. The picture (`src/ui/scene/`)
- **A doorway of the room you stand in is drawn by the sight of the room it leads to**: `SightLook` gains
  `paintDoorway(painter, palette, frame, time)` and hands on to the `DoorwayLook` it was built with — `LitDoorway` for
  `visited` (the sill and posts, the light spilling in, the number), `FoggedDoorway` for `known` and `fog` (the frame,
  the fog, the slow pulse). The way out is a `LitDoorway` in its own ink (`ab`) with no number; its arrow under it
  stays. Other rooms' doorways keep today's plain gap. `PlanPicture.#doorways` (`PlanPicture.ts:221-264`) hands each
  doorway on; it never asks which sight.
- **`DoorwayFrame`**: one doorway as the framing puts it — the gap's ends, the way in, the scale, the light's ink, its
  number (none for the way out) and the frame's seed, which places the fog. It paints the sill and the posts, so the
  two looks share them. Sizes are in plan units, so the same code draws the small doorway on the whole plan.
- **The light's ink**: `RoomLight` gains `ink()` (`NoLight` answers `cy`, the frame's); `RoomInsides` answers the ink
  for a light's key — its table (`ScenePictures.ts:254-262`) stays the one owner.
- **The ring** round a lit doorway (the twin's light, `PlanPicture.ts:249-263`) stays.
- **The name** of the room you stand in is not written (`PlacedRoom.#write`); `DrawnInside.nameLine` goes if nothing
  then asks it.
- **The MAP key leaves the picture**: `PlanScene` still owns it and its mode, and mounts it where its parts say
  (`PlanSceneParts.keys`, a `KeySlot`) — a static, empty element of the strip, as the canvases' hosts are
  (`HudView.ts:497-502`); today it builds it over its host (`PlanScene.ts:108-120`, which `HOUSEKEEPING.md` asks to
  mount through the parts). The card hears its tap as a click bubbling from the slot and shows the front.
- **The relic's flight** lands on what the screen marks as the buffer's landing; the attribute's name has one owner, a
  constant beside `StatKey` that the view writes and `RelicFlight` reads in place of its own selector
  (`RelicFlight.ts:9`).

### 3. The screen (`src/ui/screens/`, `src/ui/card/`, `app.css`)
- **Where the moves sit**: a third `MovesPlace`, `MovesOnCard`, for the plan (in place of `MovesInDock`, which goes with
  its last user). `MovesLayout` gains `ways`, shown or not; the presenter builds the card when the drawing says so and
  never asks the kind.
- **`RoomCardVM`** (`HudVM.card`, a `Panel`): the corner's two words and what a reader hears, the arrival's first
  paragraph (`place.description[0]`, never cut), the ways (the moves not on the strip), the game's options not on the
  strip, and the keys — Buffer with its count, Trace, and the way back: the way out where it is offered, else the
  engine's back move by its id. On a room `dock` and `moves` are empty. Words and casing are the presenter's.
- **`RoomCardView`** (`src/ui/screens/RoomCardView.ts`, so the words wall reads it): the card's markup and its one
  piece of state, which face shows. The front holds the picture, the corner, and one line over the picture: the
  place's kind and name — the screen's one `place-kind`, `place-name` and heading, kept in the markup for a reader and
  faded for the eye — with the first paragraph on arrival, or the step's status (the one `status` element) when it has
  one. The back holds the chips, the words, the furniture, the relic tiles, the ways, the game's options, then the scan,
  map and telemetry panels as they are. The trace column stays outside the card, over everything, as today
  (`HudView.ts:519-520`). The face turned away is `visibility: hidden`: no finger and no reader reaches a hidden
  button. The corner is a real button with `aria-pressed`. A new room, the Map key and a doorway's pick show the front.
  It is handed `ReducedMotion` and `CardTurns` in `main.ts`; `HudView` hands it its part of the template and grows by
  nothing else (`HOUSEKEEPING.md` already names it as holding too much).
- **Focus** (`Shell`): each face has a resting place (`data-rest`); the back is shown before `render` returns on a step
  that brings a panel, so the panel's `data-spot` is visible when the shell reaches it; the shell's "shown button"
  (`Shell.ts:123-125`) also asks the element's visibility, so the focus never lands on the hidden face.
- **The strip**: Buffer, the Map slot, Trace, the way back — real `button[data-option]`s but for Map, each ≥ 44 px with
  a small drawn icon and its word. The dock is not rendered on a room; the debug fold stays last, under the strip.
- **The layout**: top bar, rail, the card filling the rest, the strip — one screen at 360 × 640, the page's scroll left
  only for the debug fold and the build line. The hook `.world[data-drawn]:has(.objects)` (`app.css:1735-1800`)
  becomes an attribute the view writes; the peek button and its styles go.
- **The turns** (`src/ui/card/`): `CardTurn` — one way the card turns: it plays on the two faces and says when it ends.
  `FlipTurn`, `DoorTurn`, `PeelTurn`, `BlindsTurn`, `StaticTurn`, `TornTurn`, and `InstantTurn` for reduced motion and
  for a panel. Each plays through the browser's own animations (`Element.animate`, measured by elapsed time, as
  `RelicFlight` does), so it needs no clock of its own. `CardTurns` holds them in two lists and picks one from the
  frame's seed (the drawing's `noise`, branched by the turn's count since the step), the `decay` and the turn before,
  which it never repeats; nothing reads `Math.random` or the clock. The static's grain is one small tile on the
  canvases' pixel budget (`GrainTile` in `src/ui/canvas/`, built from the canvas maker and the same seed); the torn
  bands come from the seed too. All last as long as the flip; taps wait while one plays.
