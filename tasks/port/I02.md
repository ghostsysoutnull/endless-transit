# I02 — The big world

**Plan (as run):** content lists → domain values → the lazy `Location` tree + one factory per kind in a registry →
save = seed + path → `Journey` + the travel commands → the first HUD and a shell that switches screens → browser tests
in both profiles → housekeeping + this note. One commit per module, tests first. A rewrite guided by the Groovy source:
the rules and numbers are kept, none of the designs of study §2.2 is (no closures as menus, no list interception, no
static state, no UI call from the model, order = the index file's order).

## Guide-vs-code disagreements (Decision 6: the Guide wins)
1. **A planet's culture.** Code: drawn from all ten cultures of the index, `abyssal` included, which has no frame colour
   and falls back to white (`ThemeService.groovy:67`, `PlanetFactory.groovy:59`). Guide :348 lists nine frame colours and
   :473 keeps the abyssal words for below bedrock. **Done the Guide's way:** planets draw from the nine cultures of
   `themes/planet-frames.txt`; `abyssal` waits for the bedrock (I07). Pinned: `WorldRanges.test.ts`, "nine surface cultures".
2. **Where the game starts.** Guide :41 "You start on a **street**" (code agrees: `WorldGenesis` deep start). **Not done
   this way yet, on the brief's word:** I02 starts at the universe, because the street is a sealed bottom until I03 — a
   first screen with nothing to enter would be the dead end the brief forbids. The deep start is one line in
   `Journey.enter` and belongs to I03, when a street has doors. *This is the one place the Guide does not win yet.*
3. **Second culture / era.** Code retries up to ten times for a different one and may still end equal; mine draws from
   the list without the first, so it always differs. The Guide (:307) assumes two different ones. Rule kept, edge removed.
4. **When a rebel city or a country's mutation is decided.** Code decides both inside `populate` — so a city's own
   description could be drawn before it knew it was rebel. Mine decides at creation. Not player-visible in the Guide.

## Choices the brief did not dictate
- **Null Reaches are in** (the queue lists "Null Sector" under I03): without them 30% of filament nodes would be the
  wrong kind and the landmark rule could not be true. Their echo scan and capture stay for the items iteration.
- **Place names became content** (`names/<kind>/…`, 32 new files, each directory with its `index.txt`); in Groovy they are
  literals in `NameGenerator`. Words are content; order is the index's. Same for `themes/traits.txt`.
- **A building exists only as a name, a size and `sealed()`**, with a registered factory that populates nothing. I03
  removes `sealed()` and fills `populate` — no other file should need to change.
- **A sealed row is not a button** — a dashed, dim line with a `SEALED` tag and one sentence above the list. A disabled
  button that does nothing under a thumb is the "dead button" the brief rules out.
- **An option names its place** (`GameOption.place`): the HUD shows a sealed building by name, not by "Enter Building: …".
  Cutting the name out of the label in the presenter would be study §2.2's "labels parsed downstream" again.
- **Draws the kernel has no helper for** use `range`: a country's stability shift is `range(-100,100)/1000`; the
  building rarity roll is `range(0,9999)/10000`, one roll deciding landmark / uncommon / common exactly as the source.
- **Three connective sentences are mine** (solar system, planet, street have no description in Groovy beyond their
  header); I04 owns room and world text. Everything else is the source's wording.
- **Title flow:** NEW WORLD → the world is shown → ENTER WORLD / RE-ROLL. From the HUD, TITLE SCREEN keeps the place;
  the title then offers CONTINUE. A reload always lands where the traveller stood. Save v2; a v1 save is "no save"
  (Decision 15). A path that leads nowhere — or into a sealed place — is a corrupt save: fresh title, never half a world.
- **Keys** (desktop extra): children `1–9` then letters no command uses, `L` leave, `T` title, `E` enter.
- **The dock** (leave, title) is sticky at the bottom edge on a phone; on a desktop it sits under the narrative panel.
- **`terminal/CLAUDE.md` was edited** (docs only, the brief's permission): it received what the recovery prompt shed.
  No Groovy file changed.

   over half of the first screen. Readable, nothing overflows; I09 owns phone fine-tuning.

**The tester can try:** open the page on a phone held upright and on a desktop. Tap **NEW WORLD**, then **ENTER
WORLD**: you stand in the universe. Tap a filament, then keep tapping the first thing on each list — sector (or a
**Null Reach**, "VOID_REACH" on the list), solar system, planet, country, city, street: eight levels. The top box shows
the path you took and how deep you are; from the planet down the frame takes the planet's colour and the street header
tells its era and culture. On the street the buildings are listed with dashed borders and a **SEALED** tag — they open
in the next build; a landmark shows in bold cyan. **▲ LEAVE …** at the bottom edge climbs one level, all the way back
to the universe. In a city with many streets, scroll: LEAVE stays under your thumb, and the next place opens from its
top. Close the tab anywhere and come back — you are where you were. **TITLE SCREEN** then **CONTINUE** returns to it;
**RE-ROLL** is a new world. On a desktop: digits and letters pick a place, `L` leaves, `T` is the title. Nothing should
scroll sideways. One in ten cities is red-flagged **UNAUTHORIZED_ZONE** — its streets show the planet's *other* era
and culture.
