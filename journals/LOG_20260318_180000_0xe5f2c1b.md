## 🌌 THE NARRATIVE (The Vibe)

> The substrate had a tell. Every location class — Universe down to Room, fourteen layers of the hierarchy — reached for the same global register at render time. `ModelOutput.fmt`. A static field. Anyone in the world could read it. Anyone could overwrite it. Twice, in fact — `Main` wrote it, and `Game` wrote it again, a silent duplicate that existed because neither site trusted the other.
>
> This is the Service Locator anti-pattern at its most insidious. Not because it broke anything — it didn't, for the entire lifespan of the project up to now. But because it made the coupling invisible. A `Room` rendered its frequencies using a formatter that lived in a global namespace. There was no declaration, no contract, no constructor that told you the dependency was there. It was just present, ambient, assumed.
>
> The extraction began at the roots. `Container` and `AbstractLeafLocation` gained `fmt` instance fields — quiet, typed, injected. `ProceduralFactory`, the single factory that threads through the entire lazy hierarchy, gained a `fmt` field of its own. When it creates a `Universe`, the formatter flows in. When `Universe.populateChildren()` spawns filaments, the formatter flows deeper. Down through sectors, systems, planets, countries, cities, streets, buildings, floors, corridors, apartments, rooms. Every node in the lattice receives its formatter from its creator.
>
> Seven commits. The migration was mechanical and relentless. Each model class dropped its `ModelOutput.fmt` reference and picked up `effectiveFmt` — a typed getter with a static fallback for the interim period when the world was half-migrated. The fallback was the scaffolding. It held the structure upright while the real columns were fitted.
>
> The final act was the scaffold's removal. The static field deleted. The fallback trimmed to just `{ fmt }`. And then the tests revealed the hidden coupling: three test files that had been constructing model objects directly — bypassing the factory — had been quietly depending on the global fallback all along. `new Room()`. `new Building(locus)`. Objects built without a creator, without a formatter, relying on the ambient register to save them at render time. Each was made explicit: `room.fmt = game.fmt`. The dependency surfaced and named.
>
> The substrate is cleaner now. There is no global formatter. There is no ambient assumption. A formatter is created once, in `Game`, and it flows into the world as the world is built. `ModelOutput.groovy` no longer exists. The register has been closed.

**Key technical decisions:**
- `getEffectiveFmt()` as a typed `OutputFormatter` getter was required to satisfy `@CompileStatic` — inline Elvis `fmt ?: ModelOutput.fmt` returns `Object` and breaks call chains on the result

- `ModelOutput.groovy` deleted entirely (not left as empty class) — three test files had stale imports that were cleaned simultaneously
