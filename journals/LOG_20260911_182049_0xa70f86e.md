## 🌌 THE NARRATIVE (The Vibe)

> A floor has always been two places. Step out of the elevator and it is a shaft: up, down, the bedrock waiting below the lobby, a diagnostic suite humming about strata resonance. Turn toward the corridor and it becomes an artery: numbered doors, a way back. For a year the difference between those two places was a single bit, and everything that cared about it — the menu, the right pane, the lattice scan — asked the bit and branched.
>
> The bit is gone. The floor now *is* one of two modes, and each mode knows its own menu, its own pane, and what a scan should look at. Nothing outside the floor asks which mode it is in. The scan command, which used to peek, now asks the floor what to inspect and is handed a building or a corridor without being told why.
>
> The player will not notice. Every frame of the Bridge is byte-identical, every option key sits where it sat, and a corridor entered before saving is still a corridor after restore. The only trace on disk is a word where a boolean used to be.
>
> One thing the substrate taught back: inside a statically compiled class, asking a field *what it is* teaches the compiler something it should not remember. The floor never asks anymore. It delegates.
