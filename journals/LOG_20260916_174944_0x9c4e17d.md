> The gateway had a manual and a codex, and both spoke in the voice of the lattice. They told the Observer that the buffer held sixteen fragments, that ancient eras drained at half pulse, that a stabilized synthesis returned thirty. The prose was beautiful. >

>
> So this session did not write from the manual. It sent three probes into the source and asked the code, line by line, what it actually does. The code answered plainly. Coherence drains once per prompt, not per step, and a typo costs the same as a stride. Merging returns fifteen, always, even when the merge is refused. The buffer has no ceiling; the `/16` is paint. The ritual is not seventy percent of the floors and seven resonant fragments — it is *every* floor and seven merges, and the eighth merge is the one that yields the key. A door's trace has never once lied about the room behind it. The basement has no floor.
>
> And the code confessed things the manual never knew. A thirty-percent lottery that, once it pays, pays again on every prompt you stand still. A god menu behind the word `glitch`. A `--seed` flag the launcher advertises and the engine never reads. A spectral echo mini-game reachable only by a capital letter, because its lowercase key is stolen by the scan. A word list of twenty-eight haunted objects — `altar of the core-dump`, `pact of the root-user` — that only the breached ever see.
>
> The new page speaks to a *player*, not an Observer. Sentence case. "You." Every number followed by a hidden comment naming the line it was read from, so the next hand can check the substrate again instead of trusting the page. The lore pages keep their voice; this one keeps its receipts.
>
> The gateway now has two channels. One tells you what the transit feels like. The other tells you what it is.
