## 🌌 THE NARRATIVE (The Vibe)

> Step one gave the lattice words. Step two taught it not to repeat itself.
>
> The Observer answered seven questions with one word again, and the work went the way the last step had taught it to go: every change rehearsed in a scratch overlay against the thirty-six frames before a single golden was allowed to move. Six times the rehearsal said exactly which frames would change. Once it said more than the plan had — eight frames instead of two — and the chain stopped, as it was built to. The reason was already decided, not discovered: the golden city was a rebel district, and the Observer had chosen that rebel districts swap their eras as they swap their cultures. So Clockcity slid from the analog age into the digital one, and the room that had been lit by a cathode tube is lit now by a flat-panel monitor left on.
>
> Every apartment now shuffles a deck of every relic it could hold and deals from the top, so no cell in it echoes another. Furniture stopped pretending to be loot: an overturned tatami mat, a cracked neural interface, a dust-covered brass clock. One apartment in six drifts into the planet's second era and says so on its door. Cells are named for what they are, with a word from their culture in front — *Silent Fuel Depot*, *Fungal Processing Core* — and the hex serial that had been doing uniqueness's job is gone. Corridors and floors have four sentences each instead of one.
>
> And one honest number: the plan promised more distinct objects than the arithmetic allows. A deck holds two hundred and seventy-two, and seven hundred objects drawn from one deck cannot be more than two hundred and seventy-two different things. The template no longer caps the words. The lists do. That is what the third step is for.
