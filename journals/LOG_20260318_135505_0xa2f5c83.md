## 🌌 THE NARRATIVE (The Vibe)

> The substrate had a silent fault running through it for a long time. Room types were
> identified by shouting a string into the void and waiting to hear an echo. If the echo
> came back wrong — a renamed room type, a misspelled keyword, a refactored constant — the
> door would emit SILENCE where it should have emitted CLICKING, and nothing in the lattice
> would warn you. The failure was invisible. It was the kind of rot that accumulates behind
> the walls.
>
> Phase 3a sealed it. The `RoomCategory` enum is not just a refactor — it is a contract
> burned into the type system. Every door's trace is now a direct property of the category
> that spawned the room. The string matching ritual is gone. The enum speaks for itself.
>
> Phase 3b addressed a different kind of fragility: the kind where a number carries meaning
> that no number can express on its own. `int frequency` was doing double duty — it was a
> raw value AND a resonance signal AND a master-number detector AND a keystone marker. The
> logic was scattered across `Player`, `Gematria`, and `BridgeView`, each implementing their
> own fragment of the same truth. `SpectralFrequency` collects those fragments into one
> coherent waveform. `isResonant()`. `isMasterNumber()`. The meaning travels with the value.
>
> There was one surprise: the pre-check safety net tests (B5, B6, B7) that were written to
> pin the `int frequency` contract became the first thing to break when the field type
> changed. This was not a failure of the safety net — it was the safety net doing its job.
> It caught every assertion that was silently depending on `frequency` being a primitive,
> and forced each one to be re-expressed in terms of the new typed API. The migration was
> mechanical, but the test suite became more precise for having gone through it.
>
> The substrate now has two fewer primitive obsessions. The world is stricter, quieter, and
> more honest about what it knows.
